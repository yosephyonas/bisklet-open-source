# Bisklet: evidence, intended impact and adoption

Bisklet's Flutter, Laravel and Go software has been developed around Addis Ababa's growing cycling network and tested with smart bicycle locks. The internal hardware integration is not proof of a public bike-sharing operation.

## External evidence

- ITDP's *Addis Ababa NMT Implementation Plan 2025–2028* reports more than **241 km of cycle tracks** and **426 km of pedestrian walkways** across eight development corridors by July 2026: https://africa.itdp.org/publication/addis-ababa-nmt-implementation-plan-2025-2028/
- Xinhua (October 2026) reported that the separate operator **Addis Bike** had grown from 20 to 100 bicycles and reported **35,311 registrations** and **51,720 trips** since its August 2024 launch: https://chinaview.cn/africa/20261031/e05aea543f2045298526447a8b08cc7d/c.html
- ITDP's Addis Ababa Cycle Network Plan 2023–2032 describes a programme for a coherent, safe and connected network: https://africa.itdp.org/publication/addis-ababa-cycle-network-plan-2023-2032/

These figures indicate local need and early market activity. They **do not** establish Bisklet adoption.

## Open-source value and limitations

The code supplies a rider interface, mobility and administrative APIs, IoT integration patterns and automated tests. It is not a turnkey public bike-sharing service; operators need the appropriate licenses, technology rights and physical safety procedures.

**Current evidence:** Internal development, hardware integration and automated tests.

**Not established:** Independent installations, external contributors, public trips or fleet permits.

**Next milestones:** Reproducible installation, outside developer feedback, real community contributions, active maintenance, stakeholder consultation and aggregated consent-based usage reporting after an authorized pilot.
